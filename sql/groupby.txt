SQL AGGREGATE FUNCTIONS + GROUP BY + ORDER BY (UPDATED INTERVIEW DOC)

---

## SAMPLE TABLE: employees

## id | name | department | salary

1  | A    | IT         | 100
2  | B    | IT         | 200
3  | C    | HR         | 150
4  | D    | HR         | 150
5  | E    | Sales      | 300
6  | F    | Sales      | 250

---

1. COUNT() — COUNT EMPLOYEES PER DEPARTMENT

---

Query:
SELECT department, COUNT(*) AS total_employees
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY total_employees DESC;

Output:
department | total_employees
----------------------------

IT         | 2
HR         | 2
Sales      | 2

Notes:

* ORDER BY DESC → highest first
* Useful when counts differ

---

2. SUM() — TOTAL SALARY PER DEPARTMENT

---

Query:
SELECT department, SUM(salary) AS total_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY total_salary DESC;

Output:
department | total_salary
-------------------------

Sales      | 550
IT         | 300
HR         | 300

Notes:

* Very common interview question
* Sorting by aggregate is important

---

3. AVG() — AVERAGE SALARY

---

Query:
SELECT department, AVG(salary) AS avg_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY avg_salary DESC;

Output:
department | avg_salary
-----------------------

Sales      | 275
IT         | 150
HR         | 150

Notes:

* ORDER BY AVG is frequently asked

---

4. MIN() — LOWEST SALARY

---

Query:
SELECT department, MIN(salary) AS min_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY min_salary ASC;

Output:
department | min_salary
-----------------------

IT         | 100
HR         | 150
Sales      | 250

Notes:

* ASC → smallest first

---

5. MAX() — HIGHEST SALARY

---

Query:
SELECT department, MAX(salary) AS max_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY max_salary DESC;

Output:
department | max_salary
-----------------------

Sales      | 300
IT         | 200
HR         | 150

Notes:

* DESC → highest first

---

6. ALL AGGREGATES TOGETHER

---

Query:
SELECT
department,
COUNT(*) AS total_employees,
SUM(salary) AS total_salary,
AVG(salary) AS avg_salary,
MIN(salary) AS min_salary,
MAX(salary) AS max_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY total_salary DESC;

Output:
department | total | sum | avg | min | max
------------------------------------------

Sales      | 2     | 550 | 275 | 250 | 300
IT         | 2     | 300 | 150 | 100 | 200
HR         | 2     | 300 | 150 | 150 | 150

Notes:

* You can sort by ANY aggregate column

---

7. HAVING + ORDER BY

---

Query:
SELECT department, AVG(salary) AS avg_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
HAVING AVG(salary) > 200
ORDER BY avg_salary DESC;

Output:
department | avg_salary
-----------------------

Sales      | 275

Notes:

* HAVING filters first, then ORDER BY sorts

## INTERVIEW QUESTIONS + ORDER BY

1. Department with highest total salary

Query:
SELECT department, SUM(salary) AS total_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY total_salary DESC
LIMIT 1;

Output:
Sales | 550

---

2. Department with lowest average salary

Query:
SELECT department, AVG(salary) AS avg_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY avg_salary ASC
LIMIT 1;

Output:
IT | 150
(or HR depending on tie)

---

3. Sort departments by salary difference

Query:
SELECT department,
MAX(salary) - MIN(salary) AS diff
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY diff DESC;

Output:
IT     | 100
Sales  | 50
HR     | 0

---

4. Top 2 departments by employee count

Query:
SELECT department, COUNT(*) AS total
FROM employees
GROUP BY department
ORDER BY total DESC
LIMIT 2;

Output:
IT | 2
HR | 2

---

IMPORTANT NOTES (VERY IMPORTANT)

1. ORDER BY always comes AFTER GROUP BY

Execution order:
FROM → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → SELECT → ORDER BY → LIMIT

---

2. You can ORDER BY:

* column name
* alias (recommended)
* aggregate function

---

3. Common mistake:

❌ ORDER BY salary
✔ ORDER BY SUM(salary)

---

4. Multiple sorting:

ORDER BY total_salary DESC, department ASC;

---

FINAL INTERVIEW ANSWER:

"ORDER BY is used after GROUP BY to sort aggregated results, typically using aggregate aliases like SUM or AVG. It helps in ranking and identifying top or bottom groups."
